Field manual

Why this studio exists

ConceptForge lets a designer with no 3D-modelling background produce a dimensionally credible car concept in minutes, and hand it to the senior team as real geometry. You never sculpt: you make engineering decisions — wheelbase, overhangs, greenhouse, stance — and the studio builds the surfaces. Three values drive every feature:

  • Customisability — every dimension is a live parameter in real millimetres, layered with a swappable parts kit and paint system. Two designers starting from the same template should end somewhere completely different.
  • Informativeness — every slider explains itself (hover the ⓘ), every derived metric shows its formula, and the design review warns when a choice leaves the realistic envelope of the chosen segment. Using the tool is the training.
  • Scalability — designs are plain JSON spec sheets and shareable URLs; meshes export to OBJ for Blender/Maya/CAD; and the whole studio is static files on GitHub Pages, so rolling it out to 5 or 500 recruits costs the same: nothing.

Workflow (the 6 steps we onboard recruits with)

  1. Pick a segment. Each template carries the proportions of its real-world class.
  2. Seed from a benchmark if you want to start on the exact stance of a production car.
  3. Set the chassis — wheelbase and overhangs are 80 % of a car's character. Watch the wheelbase-to-length ratio in the copilot.
  4. Shape body & greenhouse — beltline, roofline, windshield rake, tumblehome. Toggle the scale figure and dimension lines while you work.
  5. Stance and components — wheels, arch gap, spoilers, rails. Small arch gaps read “planted”; big ones read “off-road”.
  6. Review and export — clear the design-review warnings you don't intend to keep (breaking a rule on purpose is design; breaking it unknowingly is a mistake), then export.

Formulas behind the derived metrics

  • Frontal area A ≈ 0.85 × width × height — the standard automotive approximation for projected area.
  • Drag estimate Cd is a heuristic: a 0.24 baseline plus penalties for an upright windshield, a squareback tail, ride height and a boxy greenhouse. It ranks your own iterations against each other; it is not a wind tunnel.
  • Approach / departure angle ≈ atan( lowest bumper point ÷ horizontal distance to the tyre contact patch ).
  • Turning circle ≈ 2 × ( wheelbase ÷ sin 33° + track ÷ 2 ), kerb-to-kerb, assuming a typical 33° steering lock.
  • Concept mass ≈ bounding volume × a per-segment density factor calibrated to the benchmark cars. A packaging-stage guess, ±10 %.

Data sources

Segment templates and benchmark tables are derived from manufacturer-published exterior dimensions (Porsche, Toyota, BMW, Volkswagen, Tesla, Honda, Ford, Hyundai, Mazda, Audi, Volvo press specifications) for the vehicles named in each card. They are used as dimensional ground truth only — no manufacturer geometry is included; every surface is generated parametrically.

Extending the studio (for the tools team)

Everything is data-driven from data.js: a new vehicle segment is one object in SEGMENTS (defaults + benchmarks + norm bands), a new slider is one row in PARAM_GROUPS (the UI, glossary tooltip, spec sheet and share link pick it up automatically), a new wheel or spoiler is one entry in PART_OPTIONS plus a builder case in app.js. No build step, no dependencies beyond the vendored three.js.
